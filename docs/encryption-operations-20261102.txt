Encryption operations and acceptance status, 2026-11-02

Protection scope
Production PostgreSQL data and both Redis data directories reside on LUKS2.
Selected text/JSON fields also use application AES-256-GCM: see
src/security/health-field-specs.js. IVR uses authenticated envelopes in
doctor_ivr_*.*_enc columns. Numeric measurements, many SQL filter columns and
other JSON fields are not all encrypted at application level. LUKS protects
stored blocks, not a compromised running process/root or an authorized DB read.
Do not claim end-to-end encryption or application encryption of every column.

OpenBao and readiness
OpenBao remains a single Shamir node, threshold 3 of 5, on VPS 103. Its storage
is outside the CRM LUKS volume, so the volume does not contain its own unlock
service. Applications keep keys in memory for at most 300 seconds after the
last successful refresh. Readiness checks must fail after those keys expire;
liveness stays separate so a health checker does not erase a useful cache by
restarting the application during a key-service outage.

The independent operations timer checks seal status, readiness and backup
freshness once a minute, reporting technical incident codes to the existing
Sentry project. Never attach application payloads, customer identifiers,
credentials, keys or unseal shares. Confirm notification routing in Sentry;
an accepted ingest response alone does not prove a human received a message.

Backups
Main/Doctor/OpenBao keep their existing daily encrypted jobs. CRM adds an
hourly age-encrypted custom PostgreSQL backup, with at least two copies and
14-day retention. Shared configuration archives are now streamed through age.
Private age identities stay off VPS. Legacy clear files may be retired only
after the encrypted replacement is copied off-host, decrypted there and its
digest matches. Deleting a file is not proof of erasing cloud snapshots or SSD
blocks; obtain those lifecycle details from the VPS provider.

Recovery objectives
Engineering targets with successful jobs are a backup age under 26 hours for
main/Doctor/OpenBao and under 2 hours for CRM. These are monitoring thresholds,
not a contractual RPO/RTO. The owner must approve business recovery objectives.
Record measured restore time from the actual rehearsal. Off-host backup transfer
and separate recovery material are necessary for loss of an entire VPS.

Cold-boot acceptance procedure (maintenance window required)
1. Confirm fresh encrypted DB backups, OpenBao snapshot, key bundles, LUKS
   headers and certificates; validate an isolated restore before stopping a VPS.
2. Confirm an authorized operator can access offline recovery and can unseal
   through scripts/openbao-operator.py with asinu-ops SSH key authentication.
3. Reboot VPS 103 first. OpenBao is expected to remain sealed with Shamir.
   Confirm the alert, unseal manually, then verify storage guard, CRM/n8n data
   mounts, DB/Redis, API/worker and read-only business checks.
4. With OpenBao healthy, reboot VPS 36; verify mount identity before DB/API
   startup, key readiness, encrypted reads and the supervised check-in workflow.
5. Record timestamps, downtime, failures and recovery actions. Do not mark the
   whole-VPS reboot criterion passed after a service-only restart or mock test.

Outstanding owner/admin decisions
Independent KMS/HSM identity is required for a defensible auto-unseal design.
Three independent voting nodes are needed for Raft to tolerate one node loss;
two voters on two VPS do not provide that availability. Do not store a quorum
of Shamir shares on either server as an auto-unseal workaround.
Assign share holders and perform a controlled rekey after custody is agreed;
splitting copies of an existing combined recovery bundle does not revoke prior
copies. The current limited operator can rotate Transit versions but cannot
update auto_rotate_period or grant policies; an authorized OpenBao admin session
is needed for those settings. Never persist a root token to solve this.
